Abstract
The Smart Asset Management System is a web-based solution designed to improve the
management of ICT assets such as laptops, desktop computers, printers, and other technological
equipment within an organisation. Many institutions still rely on manual methods, including
spreadsheets and paper records, which can make asset tracking difficult and increase the risk of
loss, mismanagement and inaccurate recordkeeping. The proposed system seeks to address these
challenges by providing a centralised platform for recording, monitoring and managing assets
throughout their lifecycle.
The system will be developed using PHP, MySQL, HTML, CSS, JavaScript and XAMPP. It will
support key functions such as asset registration, assignment tracking, user and role management,
status monitoring and report generation.
To enhance security, the system will implement Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) through email
verification using PHPMailer. Additionally, will also introduce machine learning features to make
the system more intelligent. These features will support predictive maintenance, smart asset
allocation, asset lifecycle prediction, anomaly detection and automated alerts. By analysing
information such as device age, repair history, warranty status, usage patterns and assignment
records, the system will help administrators identify high-risk assets and make better decisions
before problems become serious.
Overall, the Smart Asset Management System aims to improve accountability, reduce asset loss,
support timely maintenance, strengthen security and simplify the day-to-day management of ICT
resources within an organisation.
Keywords: Smart Asset Management System, ICT Asset Management, Asset Tracking,
Predictive Maintenance, Machine Learning, Asset Lifecycle Management, Two-Factor
Authentication (2FA), Anomaly Detection, Asset Allocation, Report Generation, PHP, MySQL.

Chapter 1: Introduction
1.1 Background Information
Organisations today depend heavily on ICT assets such as laptops, desktop computers, printers,
networking devices, and related equipment to support daily operations (Weill & Ross, 2004). As
these assets increase in number, managing them through manual records becomes more difficult.
Administrators must keep track of who has each device, where it is located, its condition, its
maintenance history, and whether it is still useful to the organisation.
In many institutions, asset records are still maintained using spreadsheets, paper files, or isolated
systems that do not communicate effectively, resulting in inefficiencies and data inconsistencies
(ISO/IEC 19770-1, 2017). While these methods may work when the number of assets is small,
they become unreliable as the organisation grows. Records can become outdated, devices can be
misplaced, and it may take a long time to identify which assets need repair, replacement, or
reassignment.
Manual asset management also creates accountability challenges. When device assignment records
are incomplete or inaccurate, it becomes hard to know who is responsible for a particular asset.
This can lead to asset loss, unauthorised use, delayed maintenance, and poor use of organisational
resources. Administrators may also spend too much time physically checking devices and updating
records instead of focusing on more strategic ICT support tasks.
A centralised web-based asset management system can address many of these challenges by
providing a single platform for recording, updating, securing, and monitoring asset information.
Such a system improves visibility, reduces duplication, and makes it easier for administrators to
generate reports and make timely decisions.
This project goes beyond basic asset tracking by introducing machine learning features into the
proposed system. Machine learning techniques have increasingly been applied to predict
equipment failures, optimise maintenance schedules, and support resource allocation decisions
(Carvalho et al., 2019), detect unusual activity, estimate asset lifecycle stages, and generate alerts
for issues such as warranty expiry, maintenance needs, or suspicious access attempts. In this way,
the system will support both operational efficiency and proactive decision-making.

1.2 Problem Statement
Organisations are expected to manage ICT assets in ways that promote accountability, continuity
of operations, and effective use of resources. Ideally, an asset management system should provide
accurate asset tracking, secure device assignment to users, real-time status monitoring,
maintenance scheduling, lifecycle visibility, and reliable reporting.
However, many organisations still rely on manual or semi-digital methods such as spreadsheets,
paper-based records, and disconnected tools. These methods are prone to human error and do not
provide real-time visibility into asset location, condition, assignment, or maintenance needs. As a
result, organisations may experience asset loss, delayed repairs, inaccurate records, poor
accountability and unauthorised use of ICT equipment.
Existing asset management approaches also tend to lack intelligent automation. They may record
asset information, but they often do not help administrators predict which devices are likely to fail,
which assets are approaching the end of their useful life, or which user activities appear suspicious.
This limits the organisation's ability to act early and make data-driven decisions.
This project, therefore, seeks to develop a Smart Asset Management System that automates asset
tracking and management while integrating machine learning capabilities for predictive
maintenance, smart asset allocation, anomaly detection, lifecycle prediction, and automated alerts.

2.2 Current State of ICT Asset Management
ICT asset management is a process of identifying, assigning, monitoring, maintaining and retiring
technology assets within an organisation.
Many organisations are still handling their ICT assets manually with records, spreadsheets and
disjointed digital tools. These are well-known techniques and can be readily applied, but they are
ineffective when dealing with many assets. Records can be duplicated, assets not updated when it
needs to be, admin may be unable to verify if a device is active, faulty, assigned, unassigned, under
repair or retired.
The ICT asset management landscape today reveals a need for a unified system with the ability to
offer real-time visibility and accountability. This will help minimise the chance of losing asset
records and reports and simplify device ownership tracking and planning maintenance. But most
of the current systems are primarily designed to store and retrieve information and not solve future
problems or to aid intelligent decision-making.
2.3 Challenges in ICT Asset Management
Organisations face several challenges when ICT assets are managed manually or through weak
digital processes. One of the main challenges is inaccurate record-keeping. When administrators
7

=== PAGE 17 ===
rely on spreadsheets or paper records, it is easy for asset details to become outdated, incomplete,
or inconsistent. This can lead to confusion about asset ownership, location and condition.
Another challenge is poor accountability. If an asset is assigned to a user but the assignment is not
properly recorded, it becomes difficult to determine who is responsible when the device is lost,
damaged or returned in poor condition. This can increase asset misuse and reduce trust in the asset
management process.
Maintenance is also a common problem, since most organisations respond only after a device has
failed. This reactive approach increases downtime and may lead to higher repair costs. Without
maintenance history and predictive alerts, administrators may not know which assets are likely to
fail soon or which devices are approaching the end of their useful life.
Security is another concern in the system. The asset management system contains sensitive data
about users, devices, departments and assignments. If access is not properly controlled,
unauthorised users may view, change or delete important records. For this reason, secure
authentication, role-based access control and activity monitoring are important parts of a reliable
asset management system.
2.4 Related Applications, Solutions and Machine Learning Applications
2.4.1 Spreadsheet-Based Asset Tracking
Spreadsheets are frequently used to track assets as they are easy, inexpensive and familiar to most
people. They can hold asset name, serial number, user assigned, purchase date and status data. At
the start, in small organisations, spreadsheets might suffice.
However, there are some disadvantages to asset tracking using a spreadsheet. It offers little access
control, automated alerts, real-time updates, or audit trails. Spreadsheets can be incorrectly copied,
edited or shared with unauthorised users. The more assets, the greater the difficulty in managing
this approach and the likelihood of errors.
2.4.2 Snipe-IT
Snipe-IT is an open-source asset management system that can be used to track hardware, licenses,
check-in and check-out, users and reporting. This is helpful for organisations that require a formal
8

=== PAGE 18 ===
method to handle assets and boost accountability. It benefits from asset records in a single place,
audit assistance and easy assignment history.
It has assets and administrative reporting as its primary features, but it's not a complete system for
all things. It does not offer out-of-the-box machine learning capabilities for predictive
maintenance, intelligent asset allocation, anomaly detection or lifecycle prediction. This provides
a chance to build a system which integrates regular asset management with intelligent prediction
and decision support.
2.4.3 GLPI
GLPI is an IT service management and asset management platform which supports IT resource
tracking, ticketing, helpdesk functions and inventory management. It is appropriate for companies
that require troubleshooting with users and managing IT assets from a single place.
While GLPI offers numerous valuable capabilities, it can be more intricate than required for
organisations that require only targeted ICT asset management. It could also need further setup or
integration to accommodate predictions based on machine learning. The goal of this project is to
develop a more targeted system that takes the link between asset management and predictive
analytics one step further.

2.4.4 Machine Learning in Predictive Maintenance (Solution)
Machine learning has proven to be of great value in maintenance, as it can detect trends in historical
data and use that data to make forecasts. Machine learning can use these data assets: age, repair
frequency, asset warranty status, asset assignment duration, and history of asset faults, to determine
if an asset is likely to need maintenance soon.
11

=== PAGE 21 ===
Predictive maintenance becomes crucial because it paves the way for organisations to shift from
reactive to proactive maintenance. Rather than waiting for a device to fail, an administrator can be
alerted to the failure and make plans to repair or replace the device before it disrupts operations.
This can help minimise downtime, boost productivity, and cut down on unexpected repair
expenses.
2.4.5 Machine Learning in Anomaly Detection and Security (Solution)
Machine learning can also be used in security to identify what is considered unusual in the
behaviour or actions of the users or in the assets. For instance, repeated unsuccessful login
attempts, unusual login time, unexpected changes in asset status, or unusual assignment activity
may be flagged. Warning messages can assist administrators in acting in time before malicious
activities inflict serious harm.
Anomaly detection will be used in this study to aid the security component of the Smart Asset
Management System. It will not supplant regular authentication controls; it will reinforce the
normal controls by detecting activities that seem irregular and unique to normal system activities.
2.4.6 API-based Integration of Web Systems and Machine Learning Models (Solution)
In modern systems, the web application and machine learning systems are frequently separated.
This way, the system is easier to maintain; the web app can maintain user interfaces, records,
authentication, and reports; the machine learning service can do the predictions and analytics.
The proposed system will involve the PHP and MySQL application to handle the major roles in
asset management and the machine learning models will be created by utilising the Python libraries
like scikit-learn, Pandas, and NumPy. The two parts of the system will be linked together by a
Flask API. The PHP application will submit details of the asset being monitored to the Flask API,
which will respond with predictions and/or alerts that will be displayed to the administrator in real-
time.
2.5 Gaps in Existing Applications, Solutions and ML Implementations
Looking at the tools and approaches currently available, a few clear gaps stand out that are worth
addressing.
For starters, most asset management tools are built around recording and tracking they help you
stay organised and keep inventory in check, but they don't do much to help you think ahead.
12

=== PAGE 22 ===
Administrators are often left manually combining data to figure out when something needs
maintenance or replacement, which is time-consuming and leaves a lot of room for error (Laudon
& Laudon, 2022; ISO 55000, 2014).
On top of that, many of the more capable solutions on the market are built for a different audience.
They come loaded with enterprise-grade IT service management features that, while impressive,
can feel overwhelming for smaller organisations that just need something straightforward and easy
to use (Alemzadeh et al., 2018).
There's also a noticeable absence of smarter functionality in most conventional systems. Things
like predictive maintenance, intelligent asset allocation, anomaly detection, and lifecycle
forecasting rarely come built in. When they do exist, they typically require separate tools, custom
configurations, or third-party platforms all of which add complexity and cost to an already
involved process (Aggarwal, 2015; Géron, 2022).
Finally, security doesn't always get the attention it deserves. Systems that hold asset and user data
need robust protections things like strong authentication and role-based access controls. Without
them, the door is left open for unauthorised access or accidental tampering with records (Stallings,
2018; NIST, 2020).
Taken together, these gaps make a compelling case for a Smart Asset Management System one
that's secure, focused, and genuinely easy to work with, while bringing modern machine learning
capabilities into the mix. The proposed system aims to do exactly that, bringing asset registration,
assignment, tracking, user management, Two-Factor Authentication (2FA), predictive analytics,
and automated alerts together under one roof (Géron, 2022; Laudon & Laudon, 2022).

2.6.1 Inputs
The main inputs will include asset details, user details, department information, assignment
records, asset status, maintenance history, warranty information, and system activity logs. These
inputs will provide the data needed for both normal asset management and machine learning
analysis.
2.6.2 Core Application Processes
The PHP and MySQL-based web application will handle the main system processes. These include
user login, Two-Factor Authentication, role-based access control, asset registration, asset
assignment, status updates, report generation, and alert display. This layer will ensure that
administrators can manage ICT assets in a centralised and secure way.
2.6.3 Machine Learning Processing
The machine learning component will be developed using Python libraries such as Scikit-learn,
Pandas, and NumPy. Pandas and NumPy will support data preparation and analysis, while Scikit-
learn will support model development. The models will analyse asset data to support predictive
maintenance, asset lifecycle prediction, smart allocation, and anomaly detection.
2.6.4 Flask API Integration
The Flask API will act as the communication bridge between the PHP web application and the
Python machine learning models. When the system needs a prediction, the PHP application will
send relevant data to the Flask API. The API will process the request using the trained machine
learning model and return the result to the web application.
2.6.5 Outputs
The expected outputs will include accurate asset records, assignment reports, maintenance alerts,
lifecycle predictions, anomaly notifications, and decision-support reports. These outputs will help
administrators manage assets more efficiently, improve accountability, reduce downtime, and
make better decisions about maintenance and replacement.

3.2.1 Data Acquisition
Data will be collected from asset records, user assignment records, maintenance history, warranty
information, asset status updates, and login or system activity logs. Where real organisational data
is not available, realistic sample data will be prepared for development and testing purposes
(Laudon & Laudon, 2022).
3.2.2 Data Processing
The collected data will be cleaned, validated, and organised before being used by the system. This
will include removing duplicate records, handling missing values, standardising fields such as
asset status and department names, and preparing useful features such as device age, number of
repairs, warranty status, and assignment duration.
3.2.3 Model Training
Machine learning models will be trained to support predictive maintenance, lifecycle prediction,
and anomaly detection. The models may use features such as asset age, repair frequency, usage
period, status history, warranty status, and login behaviour. The most suitable model will be
selected based on the type and quality of the available data.

3.2.4 Model Validation and Testing
The trained models will be tested using separate validation data to evaluate their performance. The
project will consider measures such as accuracy, precision, recall, and error rate, depending on the

administrators.
3.3 System Development Methodology
This project adopts the Object-Oriented Analysis and Design (OOAD) paradigm to guide the
analysis, design, and implementation of the Smart Asset Management System. OOAD is
appropriate because the system consists of several independent but interconnected modules,
including Authentication and Security, User Management, Asset Registration, Asset Assignment
and Tracking, Reporting and Analytics, Alert Management, and Machine Learning-Based
Predictive Analytics. Each module can be modelled as an independent object with defined
attributes, methods, and responsibilities, promoting modularity, maintainability, and scalability.
To support iterative development, the project adopts the Agile Software Development
Methodology using the Scrum for One framework. Since the project is developed by a single
developer, Scrum for One provides a structured approach in which the developer assumes the
responsibilities of Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developer. The methodology divides
development into short sprints, with each sprint producing a functional increment of the system.
This enables continuous testing, feedback, and refinement throughout the development lifecycle.
3.3.1 Justification
Three key factors justify the selection of OOAD with Scrum for One for this project.
First, the Smart Asset Management System consists of multiple interconnected modules that must
function independently while sharing data and services. OOAD supports modular development by
allowing each component to be designed, implemented, and tested separately before integration.
Second, the project incorporates machine learning features such as predictive maintenance, asset
lifecycle prediction, anomaly detection, and smart asset allocation. The performance of these
models may require continuous experimentation, tuning, and refinement based on available asset
data. Agile development supports such evolving requirements through iterative development
cycles.

Third, Scrum for One provides a structured mechanism for planning, development, testing, and
evaluation while maintaining flexibility. This is particularly important because system
requirements and machine learning model performance may change as development progresses.
The use of Git and GitHub further supports version control, traceability, and reproducibility
throughout the project.
Traditional methodologies such as the Waterfall Model would be less suitable because they require
requirements to be fully defined before implementation begins. Since this project incorporates
predictive analytics and machine learning components that may require continuous improvement,
an iterative Agile approach is more appropriate.
